4. The system

How my Claude team makes a video now.

One prompt, and I don’t open an editing app.

  1. I send it my raw video.One take of me talking to the camera.
  2. It does the editor’s job.“You can see here it read the file. It’s going to cut it, transcribe it, look at our rule sheet, and gather real screenshots and graphics from the internet to put on there.”
  3. It writes 3 hooks.Three versions of the opening, so I can test all of them as trial reels.
  4. It storyboards first.“It storyboards it before I accept anything. All I got to do is just click approve storyboard and then the video is done.”
  5. I send edits from my phone.I don’t wait until the next day for changes.
“I didn’t touch a single editing software.”Me, Nov 19

It didn’t start this way. At 3:30 AM on Nov 15, I wrote 93 lines of instructions for my Claude editor, and it took a lot of revisions to get here. It’s worth it. I pay $200 a month for Claude, and it also builds my websites and landing pages and researches what’s going viral.

5. The rules

5 rules from 1,000 hours in Claude.

  1. Stop writing prompts. Write briefs.Talk to it like a new hire on day one: who you are, who it’s for, and what good looks like.
  2. Make it ask you 5 questions.Before it writes anything. The output gets way better.
  3. Show it. Don’t describe it.One example beats 10 adjectives.
  4. Tell it to disagree with you.If you don’t, it will tell you every idea you have is the greatest thing ever.
  5. Turn repeats into skills.Anything you explain twice, turn into a skill, so you never have to explain it again.
